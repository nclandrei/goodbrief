ERC selects 48 teams for €500 million in grants, including a project involving Romania

The European Research Council has selected 48 projects for its 2026 Synergy Grants. They include BASTRAD, a study of writing practices in the Ottoman world involving Emanuela Timotin of the Romanian Academy’s Institute of Linguistics.

The European Research Council (ERC) announced on 12 November that it had selected 48 teams for approximately €500 million in funding under its 2026 Synergy Grants. BASTRAD is the only project on the list with a host institution in Romania. Its team includes Emanuela Timotin of the Romanian Academy’s Iorgu Iordan – Alexandru Rosetti Institute of Linguistics.

In brief

  1. The ERC selected 48 of the 957 proposals submitted, around one in 20. The funding announced under Horizon Europe totals approximately €500 million.

  2. BASTRAD brings together researchers from Romania, Greece, Italy and Austria to study how multiple writing systems were used for the same language in the Ottoman world from the 14th to the 20th century.

  3. The selected projects involve institutions in 26 countries. Seventeen teams include researchers hosted outside the EU and the countries associated with Horizon Europe.

  4. Synergy Grants bring together two to four principal investigators. Base funding can reach €10 million over six years, with up to €4 million more available for certain eligible costs.

BASTRAD studies writing practices in the Balkans and Anatolia from the 14th to the 20th century. The team will examine manuscripts and printed works to understand how different alphabets were used to write the same language in the Ottoman world. Languages studied include Greek, Turkish and Eastern Romance languages, while scripts include Cyrillic, Arabic, Greek and Latin. Alongside Emanuela Timotin, the ERC lists Dimitrios Kousouris of Greece’s National Hellenic Research Foundation, Matthias Kappler of Ca’ Foscari University of Venice and Yavuz Köse of the University of Vienna.

UEFISCDI, Romania’s research funding agency, said this was the first time a principal investigator affiliated with an institution in Romania had been selected for an ERC Synergy Grant. The agency put the funding for the entire project at nearly €10 million. The ERC list identifies the host institutions at the time of application and gives no breakdown of how the budget is divided among them.

The call closed on 5 November 2025 with 957 proposals, the highest number since the scheme began in 2012. The ERC reported a 34% increase from the previous competition. Around 5% of proposals were selected, and the announced average funding is about €10.4 million per project.

Synergy Grants support research that requires complementary expertise from several principal investigators. Teams can propose projects in any field, and the ERC evaluates them on scientific excellence, including what the collaboration contributes to the project. One principal investigator may be hosted outside the EU and the countries associated with Horizon Europe. The corresponding principal investigator, who represents the team in dealings with the ERC, must be hosted in the EU or an associated country.

On top of the base ceiling of €10 million over six years, teams can request up to €4 million for eligible costs such as major equipment, access to research facilities or experimental and field work. The next steps after selection include preparing grant agreements and completing any applicable ethics checks.
