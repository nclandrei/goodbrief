1. Introduction  
1.1.  Information on the European Institute of Innovation and Technology (EIT) 
European Institute of Innovation and Technology (EIT) 
The European Institute of Innovation and Technology (EIT) is a European Union body with a legal 
personality and a wide legal capacity accorded to legal persons under national law. The EIT was created in 
2008 by the European Union (EU) to enhance Europe's global competitiveness by fostering collaboration 
between businesses, research institutions, and higher education organisations. 
The EIT is governed by Regulation (EU) 2021/695 (EIT Regulation)1, which aligns the EIT’s mission with the 
EU’s priorities and the objectives of Horizon Europe on evolving research, innovation, economic growth, 
job creation, global competitiveness, sustainable growth, higher education and entrepreneurship, by 
means of t he Knowledge and Innovation Communities (KICs), which address specific societal challenges 
and are established and supported by the EIT.  
The EIT Strategic Innovation Agenda (SIA) 2021 -2027 is aligned with Horizon Europe and lays down the 
priority fields and the strategy of the EIT for future initiatives 2, capacity to generate the best innovation 
added-value, objectives, key actions, mode of operation, expected results, impact, as well as an estimate 
of the resources needed for the duration of Horizon Europe.  
Horizon Europe Regulation 
The Horizon Europe Regulation (EU) 2021/695 foresees that the EIT takes part in the implementation of 
the Horizon Europe Programme in accordance with its strategic objectives for the period 2021 to 20273, as 
outlined in the Strategic Innovation Agenda of the EIT, and taking into account the strategic planning of 
Horizon Europe.  
EIT and Knowledge and Innovation Community (KIC) relations 
The EIT Regulation defines KICs as large-scale Institutionalised European Partnerships of higher education 
institutions, research organisations, companies and other stakeholders in the innovation process in the 
form of a strategic network, regardless of it s legal form, based on joint mid - to long -term innovation 
planning to meet the EIT’s challenges and contribute to attaining the objectives established.  
According to the EIT Regulation, and without prejudice to the Partnership Agreements and grant 
agreements between the EIT and each KIC, the KICs have substantial autonomy to establish their internal 
organisation and composition, as well as their agenda and working methods, provided that they result in

Contractual framework between the EIT and the KICs 
The long-term relations between the EIT and each KIC are based on a seven-year Partnership Agreement 
(PA)4 laying down the general terms and conditions under which the KIC operates as an Institutionalised

implementation of the KIC activities (KIC Business Plan, Cross -KIC activities, etc.) through grants, on an 
annual or multi-annual basis of up to three years with the KICs. Business plans describe the main objectives 
and expected results and actions taken by the KICs.  
1.2.  Information on the EIT Higher Education Initiative (EIT HEI) 
The EIT Higher Education Initiative (hereinafter referred to as the "EIT HEI” or “the initiative”), established 
under the EIT Strategic Innovation Agenda 2021 -20276, aims to strengthen European higher education’s 
role in innovation ecosystems. By building institutional innovation and entrepreneurship capacity, the EIT 
Higher Education Initiative  fosters systemic change in  higher education institutions  (HEIs) and enhances 
their integration into regional and European innovation networks. 
EIT HEI projects follow the Knowledge Triangle Model (see Section 3.3 for more information), described in 
the EIT Strategic Innovation Agenda, bringing together education, research, and business to co -create 
impactful solutions to global challenges. Central to the initiative is the creation of a dynamic and inclusive 
innovation community that connects HEIs, businesses, investors, and other stakeh olders, amplifying 
individual and collective project impact.

2. EIT HEI Call 2026: Intervention Logic and General Requirements 
2.1.  Scope 
Europe's future competitiveness, technological leadership and strategic autonomy depend on its ability to 
turn STEM talent, research, knowledge and technologies into innovation, entrepreneurship and industrial 
and societal impact. This requires stronger co nnections between higher education, research, skills, 
technology development, industry and markets, particularly in deep -tech innovation and the strategic  
technologies underpinning Europe's industrial competitiveness 15. The Competitiveness Compass 16, the 
European Competitiveness Agenda 17 and the New European Innovation Agenda 18 all emphasise 
strengthening Europe's innovation capacity, closing the gap between research and market uptake, and 
creating the conditions for sustainable competitiveness and technological leadership. The Clean Industrial 
Deal19, the Union of Skills 20, the STEM Education Strategic Plan 21, the EU Start-up and Scale-up Strategy22 
and EU valorisation policy 23 further highlight the importance of skills, innovation, entrepreneurship and 
stronger pathways from research and knowledge to innovation, investment and economic value. 
Higher education institutions (HEIs) are central to these objectives, bringing together talent, research, 
knowledge, infrastructure and connections to innovation ecosystems. However, gaps remain in their

2.2. Policy Objectives 
EIT HEI Call 2026 pursues three interconnected policy objectives that define the changes it seeks to achieve: 
strengthen STEM talent, strengthening institutional capacity for innovation and knowledge valorisation, 
and strengthening connections between HEIs and deep-tech and manufacturing ecosystems. 
Policy Objective 1 – STEM talent for deep tech and manufacturing innovation and entrepreneurship 
Strengthen HEI capacity to develop the innovation, entrepreneurship and intellectual property (IP) 
competences of STEM talent needed to contribute to deep -tech and manufacturing innovation. This 
includes enabling students, researchers, academic staff and professional staff to apply these competences 
in real-world innovation and industrial contexts24. 
Policy Objective 2 – Strengthen HEIs as institutional drivers of innovation, from lab to market 
Strengthen the institutional structures, processes and capabilities that enable HEIs to develop, valorise, 
transfer and commercialise knowledge and technologies, support entrepreneurship and create the 
conditions for innovation to progress towards industri al application and market entry. This includes 
strengthening leadership, governance, innovation support structures and mechanisms, knowledge and 
technology transfer capabilities and institutional pathways connecting education, research and innovation.

2.4.  Intervention Framework and Funded Actions 
Project activities must be designed in response to the institutional needs, maturity , and priorities of all 
participating HEIs and must provide a clear pathway towards the objectives and expected results of the 
Call. Each HEI must complete the HEInnovate self-assessment25, with results no more than six months old 
at the point of application submission. HEInnovate provides a baseline for identifying institutional 
strengths, gaps and priorities and informs the consortium's Innovation Vision Action Plan (IVAP).

2.4.1.  HEInnovate self-assessment and identification of institutional priorities 
Each participating HEI is required to complete the  self-assessment26. Applicants should use the results to 
identify the institutional needs and priorities where EIT HEI support can generate the greatest added value. 
The results of the self -assessment should inform the development of the project proposal, which 
constitutes the consortium's IVAP. The IVAP should set out the consortium's long -term vision for 
institutional and systemic change and provide a strategic basis for the project's activities. It must 
demonstrate: 
• the key institutional needs and priorities identified through the self-assessment 
• how these needs relate to the Policy Objectives and relevant Specific Objectives and Sub-objectives 
• the activities proposed to address these needs 
• the expected outputs and results 
• how these results are expected to contribute to sustainable institutional transformation and the 
overall aim of the Call 
 
2.4.2.  EIT HEI Impact Domains and Funded Actions 
HEInnovate assesses each participating HEI as an institution, providing an eight -dimension self-diagnostic 
that informs the consortium's own roadmap and Innovation Vision Action Plan (IVAP). In contrast, the EIT 
HEI Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning (MEL) Framework operates at Initiative level, providing a common 
framework for monitoring and learning across all funded projects and cohorts 27. Within this framework, 
the five Impact Domains organise the main areas of intervention supported by the EIT HEI and enable 
evidence to be aggregated across consortia for EIT accountability and portfolio-level learning. 
The Impact Domains are not additional Policy Objectives. Rather, they provide the established intervention 
framework through which projects address their identified institutional needs and contribute to the Policy 
Objectives and Specific Objectives of the Call. Within each Domain, Funded Actions define the specific types 
of intervention that projects may select and implement according to t heir institutional context, maturity 
and priorities. A Funded Action may contribute to more than one Specific Objective or Policy Objective, 
reflecting the interconnected nature of talent development, institutional capacity and ecosystem 
integration. The Policy Objectives, Specific Objectives and Sub -objectives referenced in this section are 
specific to the EIT HEI Call 2026 and are not part of the terminology of the EIT HEI MEL Framework, which 
organises its results framework around Impact Domains, Signs of Change, Outcomes and Impact. They

Selection of Funded Actions 
All projects must select eight Funded Actions, and no more, according to the following requirements: 
• At least one Funded Action must be selected from each of Domains 1 – 4.  
• Two additional Funded Actions must be selected from Domains 1 – 4 according to the consortium's 
institutional needs and priorities.  
• Both Funded Actions under Domain 5 must be selected by all projects.  
The selection of Funded Actions must be driven by the institutional needs and priorities identified through 
the HEInnovate self-assessment and IVAP, rather than by a requirement to cover all Impact Domains or 
predefined areas of activity. Applicants must demonstrate a clear line of sight between: 
institutional needs → Policy and Specific Objectives → selected Funded Actions and activities → outputs → 
expected results → sustainable institutional change.  
The Funded Actions therefore provide the mechanism for implementing the intervention, while the Policy 
Objectives and Specific Objectives define the changes that the intervention is intended to achieve.  The 
common Impact Domain and Funded Action framework also provides continuity across EIT HEI Calls and 
cohorts, enabling consistent monitoring, portfolio-level learning and the identification, sharing and transfer 
of effective approaches and good practices.

2.5.  KPI Architecture and Requirements 
The EIT HEI Call 2026 uses the KPI architecture established in the EIT Impact Framework29. The KPIs provide 
a common quantitative basis for measuring selected outputs and results of project implementation and 
complement the broader qualitative and institutional evidence collected through the EIT HEI MEL 
Framework. 
The KPI architecture for the EIT HEI Call 2026 comprises two complementary levels: 
• Project-level EIT HEI KPIs  and EIT Core KPIs  measure selected outputs and results generated 
through funded projects and provide quantitative evidence of progress within the EIT HEI 
intervention logic. There are four mandatory EIT HEI KPIs and projects must choose and deliver on 
at least one EIT Core KPI by the end of their funding period. 
• The EIT STEM Education Strategic Plan target represents an Initiative-level contribution to a wider 
EU and EIT strategic objective. EIT HEI projects contribute to this target through activities that 
develop innovation, entrepreneurship and IP management competences among STEM learners 
and staff. 
The relationship between activities, KPIs and the intended results  of the Call is established through the 
project's intervention logic. Funded Actions are expected to generate outputs and results that contribute 
to the Call's Policy Objectives and Specific Objectives. The applicable KPIs provide quantitative evidence for 
selected elements of this pathway, while the IVAP, qualitative evidence and the MEL Framework capture 
broader institutional and ecosystem -level change that cannot be adequately represented through 
quantitative KPIs alone. 
Applicants must therefore demonstrate in their IVAP how their proposed activities are expected to 
generate the outputs and results required to achieve the applicable KPI targets and how these results 
contribute to the objectives of the Call. KPI achievemen t should be understood as evidence of progress

 Applicants must demonstrate that they have the operational capacity, expertise , and resources required 
to implement the proposed project successfully and deliver the expected results. This should be supported 
by concrete, verifiable evidence, such as identified staff with relevant qualifications and experience, 
experience in projects of comparable scope and complexity, and access to the infrastructure, networks and 
other resources required for the proposed activities. 
 
Given the scope of the Call, applicants should demonstrate that the consortium has the capacity to 
implement the combination of interventions selected in its IVAP and work plan and to contribute to the 
three Policy Objectives. Depending on the project's pr iorities, institutional context and level of maturity, 
relevant capabilities may include: 
• Talent development: innovation, entrepreneurship and IP education; interdisciplinary and 
experiential learning; mentoring; challenge -based learning; and engagement with industry and 
other innovation actors. 
• Institutional innovation capacity: institutional leadership and governance; innovation support 
structures; knowledge valorisation and technology transfer; IP management; entrepreneurship 
support; and connections between education, research and institutional innovation functions. 
• Ecosystem integration: research -to-market pathways; industry and business engagement; 
technology validation and demonstration; access to innovation infrastructure; investment 
readiness; venture development; and connections to regional, European and interna tional 
innovation ecosystems. 
Applicants should also demonstrate access to the technical, industrial and innovation expertise, 
infrastructure and networks required to implement the proposed activities effectively. Where these 
capabilities are not available within the participating HEIs, they may be provided through other consortium 
members or relevant external partnerships, as appropriate.

11.2. Ex-aequo Proposals 
Where proposals receive the same overall evaluation score and further prioritisation is required to 
establish the final ranking, the EIT HEI Call Evaluation Committee will apply the following priority order, 
starting with the highest priority factor and proceeding sequentially until a distinction can be made: 
1. Excellence: proposals will first be prioritised according to their scores for the criterion of 
Excellence. Where these scores are equal, priority will be given according to the scores for the 
criterion of Impact 2.1: Credibility and scale of the project’s expected outcomes and impacts. 
2. Portfolio-related factors: where a distinction cannot be made on the basis of the above scores, 
the EIT HEI Call Evaluation Committee may consider additional factors relevant to the strategic 
objectives of the Call, such as: 
• gender balance and other diversity considerations in innovation capacity-building activities 
• participation of non-academic sector organisations 
• geographical diversity 
• other relevant factors justified by the objectives and scope of the Call 
The factors considered and the rationale for their application will be documented in the 
Evaluation Report. 
3. Overall portfolio and synergies: if the above factors do not allow a distinction to be made, the EIT 
HEI Call Evaluation Committee may consider the overall composition of the proposed project 
portfolio, including opportunities to create positive synergies and complementarity between 
selected projects, as well as other factors directly related to the objectives of the Call. 
The factors considered and the rationale for their application will be documented in the 
Evaluation Report prepared by the EIT HEI management team.

11.4. Redress and Complaint Procedure 
Upon receipt of the evaluation results, the applicant may wish to lodge a request for redress, if there is a 
concern that there might have been a shortcoming in the way a proposal was evaluated.  The redress 
procedure is not meant to call into question the  judgement made by the independent experts who 
evaluated the proposals. The redress procedure will only consider procedural shortcomings and factual 
errors. Requests for redress should be raised within 14 days of the receipt of the evaluation report and 
should be sent to compliance@eitrawmaterials.eu. 

PAGE 47
 
 
47 
 
Requests must:  
• Be related to the evaluation process  
• Include a clear description of the grounds for the complaint  
• Be received within the time limit specified above  
 
An initial reply will be sent to complainants no later than two weeks after the deadline for redress requests. 
This initial reply will indicate when a reply will be provided. The EIT HEI Call Evaluation Committee will be 
informed about the redress and a Redress Committee, established by EIT RawMaterials, will be convened 
to examine the evaluation process for the case under consideration. 
 
If there is clear evidence of a shortcoming that could have affected the funding decision, the Redress 
Committee may decide that all or part of the proposal should be re -evaluated. Unless there is clear 
evidence of a shortcoming there will be no follow-up or re-evaluation. The Redress Committee will inform 
the EIT HEI Call Evaluation Committee of the outcome of any such re-evaluations. 
 
Please note:  
• This redress procedure is concerned with the evaluation process.  
• The Redress Committee will not call into question the judgment of the individual expert evaluators, 
whose qualifications have already been assessed and validated.  
• A re-evaluation will only be carried out if there is evidence of a shortcoming that affects the quality 
assessment of a proposal. This means, for example, that a problem relating to one evaluation 
criterion will not lead to a re-evaluation if a proposal has failed anyway on any other criterion.  
• The evaluation score following any re-evaluation will be regarded as definitive.   
• The evaluation score may be lower than the original score.  
• Only one request (i.e. the first submitted request) for redress per proposal will be considered. 
11.5. EIT KIC Allocation Procedure 
 
Once the ranking list has been finalised, the EIT HEI Call Evaluation Committee will oversee the allocation 
of successful proposals across the participating EIT KICs for project management purposes. 
 
The allocation will use the KIC alignment information provided in each proposal as a basis for matching 
successful projects with the EIT KIC best placed to support their implementation. Where possible, projects 
will be matched with a KIC whose thematic expertise, network and innovation ecosystem complement the 
project's objectives, activities and areas of focus. The allocation will also take into account  the overall 
distribution of projects across participating KICs and their capacity to manage the awarded portfolio, with 
the aim of achieving a fair and balanced distribution while maintaining meaningful alignment between 
projects and KIC capabilities. The allocation process is therefore intended to translate the KIC connections 
identified by applicants into an appropriate project-KIC match, providing each funded project with a clear
